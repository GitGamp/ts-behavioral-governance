### Part 1: Empirical Neurodevelopmental and Behavioral Studies

**Burton, M., & Giunta, K. (2025).** *Sexual extortion of Australian adolescents: Results from a national survey* (Trends & Issues in Crime and Criminal Justice No. 712). Australian Institute of Criminology. https://doi.org/10.52922/ti77819
> **Annotation:** This national survey quantifies the prevalence and gender-differentiated dynamics of adolescent sextortion, confirming that young males are disproportionately targeted for rapid financial blackmail under threat of public exposure.

**Chew, K. A., Ponsford, J., & Gould, K. R. (2024).** Addressing cyberscams and acquired brain injury ("I desperately need to know what to do"): Qualitative exploration of clinicians' and service providers' perspectives. *Journal of Medical Internet Research*, *26*, e51245. https://doi.org/10.2196/51245
> **Annotation:** This empirical study details how cognitive and executive control deficits resulting from acquired brain injuries severely compromise a young user's internal guardianship, rendering them highly vulnerable to online scams and peer manipulation.

**Hammerslag, L. R., & Gulley, J. M. (2015).** Sex differences in behavior and neural development and their role in adolescent vulnerability to substance use. *Behavioral Brain Research*, *298*(Pt A), 15–26. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbr.2015.04.008
> **Annotation:** This paper conceptualizes sex-specific vulnerability pathways in corticolimbic development, showing how males are driven toward drug use by sensation-seeking, while females are driven by stress and emotional lability.

**Heitzeg, M. M., Hardee, J. E., & Beltz, A. M. (2018).** Sex differences in the developmental neuroscience of adolescent substance use risk. *Current Opinion in Behavioral Sciences*, *23*, 21–26. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cobeha.2018.01.020
> **Annotation:** This neuroimaging review outlines the "imbalance model" of brain maturation, demonstrating how an early-maturing subcortical reward system paired with a late-developing prefrontal cortex establishes a neurobiological vulnerability to risk-taking.

**Hietamäki, J., Husso, M., Arponen, T., & Lahtinen, H.-M. (2024).** Differences between girls and boys in the disclosure of sexual violence. *Journal of Interpersonal Violence*, *39*(11-12), 2629–2654. https://doi.org/10.1177/08862605231221283
> **Annotation:** This study explores gender-differentiated disclosure rates among minors who have experienced sexual violence, highlighting the extreme cognitive isolation and underreporting that facilitates the late stages of the Two-Stage Predatory Pipeline.

**Horskykh, M. (2018).** Online grooming as a manipulative social interaction: Insights from textual analysis. *Polish Journal of English Studies*, *4*(1), 79–111.
> **Annotation:** This text uses social exchange theory and investment models to show how groomers condition victims by establishing psychological debt, illustrating the core trust-building mechanisms of the Two-Stage Predatory Pipeline.

**McAlinden, A.-M. (2018).** Peer-to-peer grooming: A reappraisal. In *Children as ‘risk’: Sexual exploitation and abuse by children and young people* (pp. 128–165). Cambridge University Press. https://doi.org/10.1017/9781316534847.005
> **Annotation:** This chapter re-conceptualizes grooming as a non-linear, peer-to-peer behavioral loop, highlighting how peer networks can facilitate predatory conditioning outside conventional adult-to-child frameworks.

**Reneses, M., Riberas-Gutiérrez, M., & Bueno-Guerra, N. (2024).** “He flattered me”. A comprehensive look into online grooming risk factors: Merging voices of victims, offenders and experts through in-depth interviews. *Cyberpsychology: Journal of Psychosocial Research on Cyberspace*, *18*(4), Article 3. https://doi.org/10.5817/CP2024-4-3
> **Annotation:** This empirical study analyzes how online groomers systematically map and exploit children's emotional and social vulnerabilities, establishing the psychological basis of the Two-Stage Predatory Pipeline.

**Wagemaker, E. (2023).** *Peers for better or for worse? Understanding susceptibility to peer influence in adolescents with mild-to-borderline intellectual disability* (Doctoral dissertation, Universiteit van Amsterdam). UvA-DARE.
> **Annotation:** This doctoral work explores the cognitive-control deficits, such as impaired response inhibition and theory of mind, that render neurodivergent youth vulnerable to peer pressure and online manipulation.

**Wagemaker, E., van Hoorn, J., & Bexkens, A. (2022).** Susceptibility to peer influence on prosocial behavior in adolescents with mild intellectual disability or borderline intellectual functioning. *Research in Developmental Disabilities*, *120*, Article 104143. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ridd.2021.104143
> **Annotation:** This empirical study highlights the heightened susceptibility of adolescents with mild-to-borderline intellectual disabilities to peer influence, validating their extreme neurobiological vulnerability to online social engineering.

**Wood, D., Crapnell, T., Lau, L., Bennett, A., Lotstein, D., Ferris, M., & Kuo, A. (2017).** Emerging adulthood as a critical stage in the life course. In N. Halfon, C. B. Forrest, R. M. Lerner, & E. M. Faustman (Eds.), *Handbook of Life Course Health Development* (pp. 123–145). Springer. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-47143-3_7
> **Annotation:** This chapter outlines the development of executive functioning and decision-making during the transition to adulthood, explaining how the sudden drop in institutional supports exposes young adult brains to severe digital risks.

### Part 2: Trust & Safety, Algorithmic Failure, and Policy Critiques

**CESIE. (2024).** *D5.3 Policy recommendations: Protecting children across digital spaces* (CESAGRAM Report). CESIE Policy Repository.
> **Annotation:** This report provides comprehensive policy recommendations for child safety in digital ecosystems, advocating for structural and rights-oriented designs that move beyond simple keyword filters.

**Chawki, M. (2025).** AI moderation and legal frameworks in child-centric social media: A case study of Roblox. *Laws*, *14*(3), Article 29. https://doi.org/10.3390/laws14030029
> **Annotation:** This legal case study documents the limitations of automated moderation on Roblox, detailing how heuristically scoped algorithms generate false positives and fail to detect the complex social context of online grooming.

**Flynn, A., Vakhitova, Z., Wheildon, L., Harris, B., & Robards, B. (2025).** Content moderation and community standards: The disconnect between platform policies, user expectations and user experiences reporting harmful and offensive content on social media. *Policy & Internet*, *17*(3), 1–17. https://doi.org/10.1002/poi3.70006
> **Annotation:** This study critiques algorithmic moderation systems for failing to interpret the social context of offensive content, forcing marginalized communities to resort to manual workarounds to bypass rigid, automated blocks.

**Forbrukerrådet. (2025).** *Click to buy (more): How fast fashion giant SHEIN uses dark patterns to push overconsumption*. Norwegian Consumer Council.
> **Annotation:** This report documents how dark patterns and deceptive interface designs bypass deliberate, rational System 2 thinking to trigger impulsive, automatic System 1 behavior.

**Friedl, P., & Morgan, J. (2024).** Decentralised content moderation. *Internet Policy Review*, *13*(2). https://doi.org/10.14763/2024.2.1754
> **Annotation:** This article explores the history and structures of decentralized online governance, illustrating how the fragmentation of safety standards across servers makes decentralized moderation vulnerable to coordinated abuse and bad-actor recidivism.

**Haime, Z., Kennedy, L., Grace, L., Cohen, R., Derges, J., & Biddle, L. (2023).** *Experiences of moderation, moderators, and moderating by online users who engage with self-harm and suicide content* (Preprint). medRxiv. https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.02.15.24302878
> **Annotation:** This qualitative analysis demonstrates algorithmic moderation failures on commercial platforms, where rigid, automated "carpet bans" remove supportive resources while failing to address nuanced or coded harmful communications.

**Kalidindi, K. (2025, May 28).** Moderating cross-platform and cross-instance abuse on decentralized networks. *TechPolicy.Press*.
> **Annotation:** This paper analyzes cross-platform and cross-instance abuse, detailing the severe structural coordination failures of decentralized networks in intercepting systemic harassment campaigns.

**Munzer, T., Milkovich, L. M., Madigan, S., Tomopoulos, S., Parga-Belinkie, J., Ajumobi, T., Cross, C., & Gerwin, D. (2026).** Digital ecosystems, children, and adolescents: Technical report. *Pediatrics*, *157*(2), e2025075321. https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2025-075321
> **Annotation:** This clinical report outlines how persuasive platform designs and deceptive dark patterns overwhelm the developing executive functions of child users, reinforcing their neurobiological vulnerability to impulsive spending and online exploitation.

**Wang, K., Clarke, L., Dreue, C.-C. J., Zhou, G., & Jacques, J. T. (2025).** Critical challenges in content moderation for people who use drugs (PWUD): Insights into online harm reduction practices from moderators. *arXiv preprint arXiv:2508.02868*.
> **Annotation:** This empirical study critiques rule-based moderation systems for failing to interpret context, showing how simple pattern-matching filters block essential life-saving information and help-seeking behaviors.

### Part 3: Legal Briefs and Litigation Materials

**Ashurst, E., & McAlinden, A.-M. (2015).** Young people, peer-to-peer grooming and sexual offending: Understanding and responding to harmful sexual behaviour within a social media society. *Probation Journal*, *62*(4), 374–388. https://doi.org/10.1177/0264550515618512
> **Annotation:** This article analyzes how smart phones and social media applications serve as the primary conduits for peer-to-peer grooming loops and sexual coercion, validating the role of digital platform designs in facilitating abuse.

**Fairplay for Kids, & National Center on Sexual Exploitation. (2026).** *Request for investigation of Roblox Corporation*. Submitted to the Federal Trade Commission.
> **Annotation:** This administrative complaint provides detailed evidence of how Roblox uses manipulative UI features, scarcity marketing, and virtual currencies to exploit children's reward sensitivity and cognitive development.

**Florida Attorney General. (2025).** *State of Florida v. Roblox Corporation* (Filing # 237498673). Florida Office of the Attorney General.
> **Annotation:** This legal complaint documents systemic algorithmic moderation failures on Roblox, detailing how understaffed moderation and inadequate filters allow predatory grooming behavior and explicit content to flourish.

**McConvey, J. R. (2026, June 23).** Arkansas lawsuit links Roblox, Discord as 'two-stage predatory pipeline'. *Biometric Update*.
> **Annotation:** This news analysis outlines the legal claims of the State of Arkansas, which formally defines the "two-stage predatory pipeline" where predators utilize unverified gaming platforms to identify children and then transition them to unmonitored messaging spaces.

**Sokolove Law. (2026, August).** *Discord & Roblox lawsuit update | Grooming & abuse*. Sokolove Law.
> **Annotation:** This litigation update details ongoing consolidated federal multidistrict litigation (MDL 3166) and products liability claims alleging negligent platform design that actively facilitates the Two-Stage Predatory Pipeline.

**University of Pittsburgh Securities Arbitration Clinic. (2025).** *Response to "The Metaverse and the Implications for the Securities Industry" request for comment*. Financial Industry Regulatory Authority.
> **Annotation:** This legal comment details the severe regulatory and compliance concerns posed by the gamification and behavioral design of virtual environments, proving that metaverse platforms exploit the cognitive limitations of developing brains.

**Wolak, J., & Finkelhor, D. (2018).** *Sextortion of minors: Characteristics and dynamics*. Crimes Against Children Research Center, University of New Hampshire.
> **Annotation:** This empirical report analyzes the demographics and mechanics of minor sextortion, highlighting how perpetrators exploit weak platform monitoring to execute rapid, coercive isolation and extortion loops.

### Part 4: Organizational Guidelines and Policy Manuals

**Allchorn, W., & Orofino, E. (2026).** *Beyond the clan: Identity formation, influence, and extremist milieux in online gaming-adjacent spaces*. Global Network on Extremism and Technology.
> **Annotation:** This paper demonstrates how gaming-adjacent social spaces are utilized by bad actors to build trust and isolate young users, reinforcing the structural threat of cross-platform recruitment loops.

**Bravehearts.** (n.d.). *Online risks, child exploitation & grooming*. Bravehearts.org.au.
> **Annotation:** This resource documents the complex experiences of child exploitation survivors, showing how online platforms facilitate grooming and non-consensual sexual image sharing.

**Droit d'Enfance, & Missing Children Europe. (2024).** *From grooming to abuse, exploitation and disappearance*. Droit d'Enfance.
> **Annotation:** This report documents survivor testimonies of online grooming, verifying that the grooming process consists of overlapping behavioral clusters that systematically exploit a child's emotional and social vulnerabilities.

**Johansen, K. B. H. (2019).** *Unwanted digital sexual attention and gendered vulnerability in a Nordic context*. Justitsministeriet.
> **Annotation:** This research explores the gendered vulnerabilities of youth navigating unsolicited digital sexual attention, illustrating how platforms normalize toxic, coercive behavioral feedback loops.

**National Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Children.** (2020). *Grooming: Recognising the signs*. NSPCC Learning.
> **Annotation:** This safeguarding brief outlines the critical indicators of grooming behaviors, illustrating how predators systematically cultivate relationships to isolate children from protective networks.

**Skills for Health.** (n.d.). *6 stages of grooming adults and teens: Spotting the red flags*. Skills for Health UK.
> **Annotation:** This clinical guide breaks down the behavioral stages of grooming, illustrating how predators utilize progressive conditioning to systematically bypass a victim's internal defenses.

**Thorn. (2025).** *Sexual extortion & young people: Navigating threats in digital environments*. Thorn.org.
> **Annotation:** This empirical report reveals the rapid, highly coercive dynamics of financial sextortion targeting young adults, validating how offenders exploit neurobiological risk-taking behaviors.
